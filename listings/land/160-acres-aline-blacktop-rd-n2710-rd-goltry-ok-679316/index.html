ONLINE ONLY — SELLING ABSOLUTE, REGARDLESS OF PRICE!!! Opportunities to own a property this diverse in southeast Alfalfa County are hard to come by. Located just 2 miles southwest of Goltry, Oklahoma, at the corner of E0350 Rd and N2710 Rd, this 160± acre parcel combines productive grassland, standout recreational ground, and steady oil income — a rare mix to find under one parcel. The land itself tells the story. Turkey Creek winds through the property alongside a second wet-weather creek, feeding roughly 30 acres of some of the densest timber you'll find for miles. With rolling terrain, natural topography changes, and creek beds cutting through mature woods — all surrounded by neighboring tillable farm production — this parcel forms a natural travel corridor and year-round home for deer. For the hunter or recreational buyer, that combination of heavy cover, water, and adjacent crop ground is exactly what holds and grows the deer. The balance of the property is laid to grass, currently used for grazing and hay production, with a solar well, cattle guard access, and multiple access points with lease roads throughout. The oil income is also notable on this property. Five oil wells operated by Superior Production LLC generate ongoing income that will transfer to the buyer. On top of the oil income, the property is leased for grazing and hay production through July 31, 2027 at $5,400. Between the oil production, the ag lease, the recreational draw, and the raw acreage itself, this is a property that offers something for nearly every kind of buyer. Definition of 'selling by the acre': A method of sale often used for agricultural or undeveloped acreage in which bids are made based on a per acre price. By way of example, if a 160-acre parcel was offered “by the acre” the high bid may be $5,000 an acre. That amount would then be multiplied times the total acreage to arrive at a total bid price of $800,000 (plus Buyer’s Premium, if applicable). For the purposes of calculating the total sales price, the acreage will be rounded to the nearest whole acre.
